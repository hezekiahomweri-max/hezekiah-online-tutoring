Free practice: Energy supplied by a heater

Use E = Pt with power in watts and time in seconds. If all supplied energy heats the stated mass without a change of state, E = mcΔT.

Worked example

A 1,000 W heater operates for 120 s to heat 2 kg of water. E = 1,000 × 120 = 120,000 J. With c = 4,200 J/(kg·°C) and no energy losses, ΔT = 120,000/(2 × 4,200) ≈ 14.3 °C.

Try it yourself

Show your working or explain your reasoning, then open the answers to check.

  1. A 500 W heater runs for 3 minutes. How much energy does it supply?
  2. A 1,200 kg car slows uniformly from 20 m/s to rest in 4 s. Find the magnitude of the resultant force.
  3. A 400-turn coil has a flux change of 4 × 10⁻⁴ Wb per turn in 0.08 s. Find the magnitude of average induced emf.
Show answers and working
  1. t = 180 s. E = 500 × 180 = 90,000 J.
  2. a = (0 − 20)/4 = −5 m/s². F = ma = −6,000 N; magnitude 6,000 N, opposite to motion.
  3. |emf| = N|ΔΦ|/Δt = 400 × 4 × 10⁻⁴/0.08 = 2 V.

Remember: The no-loss heating model gives an ideal estimate. Energy transferred to the container and surroundings reduces the water’s actual temperature rise.
