Free practice: Finding the inverse of a 2 × 2 matrix

For A = [[a, b], [c, d]], the determinant is ad − bc. When it is non-zero, A⁻¹ = (1/(ad − bc))[[d, −b], [−c, a]].

Worked example

For A = [[2, 1], [1, 1]], det(A) = 2 × 1 − 1 × 1 = 1. Its inverse is [[1, −1], [−1, 2]]. Multiplication AA⁻¹ gives [[1, 0], [0, 1]], confirming the result.

Try it yourself

Show your working or explain your reasoning, then open the answers to check.

  1. Find the determinant of [[3, 2], [1, 4]].
  2. Can [[2, 4], [1, 2]] be inverted? Explain.
  3. Find the magnitude of the vector (6, 8).
Show answers and working
  1. 3 × 4 − 2 × 1 = 10.
  2. No. Its determinant is 2 × 2 − 4 × 1 = 0.
  3. √(6² + 8²) = √100 = 10.

Remember: Check that the determinant is non-zero before dividing by it. A zero determinant means this inverse formula cannot be used.
