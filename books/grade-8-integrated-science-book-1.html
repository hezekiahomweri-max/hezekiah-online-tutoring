Free practice: Distinguishing a physical change from a chemical change

A physical change changes form or state without making a new substance. A chemical change produces one or more new substances. Give evidence for your classification.

Worked example

Melting ice is a physical change: solid water becomes liquid water, but the substance is still water. Rusting iron is a chemical change because new substances form as iron reacts with oxygen and water.

Try it yourself

Attempt the questions before opening the answers.

  1. Classify dissolving sugar in water.
  2. Classify burning paper and explain.
  3. Is pure copper an element, compound or mixture?
Show answers and working
  1. A physical change: no new substance is formed, and the sugar can be recovered by removing the water.
  2. A chemical change: new substances such as gases and ash form.
  3. An element: it contains one kind of atom.

Remember: Do not rely only on whether a change is easy to reverse. The formation of new substances is the key distinction.
