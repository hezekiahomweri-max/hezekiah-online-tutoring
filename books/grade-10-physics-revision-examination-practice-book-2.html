Free practice: Using spring-extension data

Within its proportional limit, a spring obeys F = kx. Convert extension to metres before calculating the spring constant in N/m.

Worked example

A load of 6 N extends a spring by 3 cm. Convert 3 cm to 0.03 m: k = 6/0.03 = 200 N/m. If the spring remains within its proportional limit, a 10 N load gives x = 10/200 = 0.05 m = 5 cm.

Try it yourself

Show your working or explain your reasoning, then open the answers to check.

  1. A spring extends 2 cm under a 4 N load. Find k.
  2. A fan operates at 12 V and draws 3 A. Find its electrical power.
  3. A signal travels 9 × 10⁷ m at 3 × 10⁸ m/s. Find the travel time.
Show answers and working
  1. k = 4/0.02 = 200 N/m.
  2. P = VI = 12 × 3 = 36 W.
  3. t = distance/speed = 0.3 s.

Remember: A straight-line force-extension graph supports proportionality only over the measured range. Do not assume it continues beyond the spring’s proportional limit.
