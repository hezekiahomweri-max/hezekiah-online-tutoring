Free Grade 10 Chemistry notes: atomic structure

An atom has a small nucleus containing protons and neutrons. Electrons occupy energy levels outside the nucleus. Protons have a positive charge, electrons have a negative charge, and neutrons have no charge.

  • Atomic number (Z) = number of protons.
  • Mass number (A) = number of protons + number of neutrons.
  • Number of neutrons = A − Z.
  • In a neutral atom, the number of electrons equals the number of protons.

Remember: mass number counts particles in the nucleus. It is different from relative atomic mass, which takes isotope abundance into account.

Worked example: sodium-23

A neutral sodium atom has atomic number 11 and mass number 23. It therefore has 11 protons, 11 electrons and 12 neutrons (23 − 11). Its electron arrangement is 2,8,1.

Try it yourself

  1. A neutral atom has atomic number 13 and mass number 27. Find its proton, electron and neutron numbers, then write its electron arrangement.
  2. Chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 both have atomic number 17. Explain why they are isotopes.
  3. A learner says that every sodium atom must have exactly the same mass number. Use the meaning of isotopes to assess the claim.
Show answers and explanations
  1. 13 protons, 13 electrons and 14 neutrons (27 − 13); electron arrangement 2,8,3.
  2. They have the same proton number, 17, but different neutron numbers: 18 and 20 respectively. Atoms of the same element with different neutron numbers are isotopes.
  3. The claim does not follow from the definition of an element. Sodium atoms must have 11 protons, but isotopes can have different neutron numbers and therefore different mass numbers.

Think Big: changing the neutron number changes the isotope; changing the proton number changes the element.
