Free practice: Converting mass to amount in moles

Use n = m/M, where m is mass in grams and M is molar mass in g/mol. A balanced equation then tells you the ratio of reacting amounts.

Worked example

Calculate the amount in 5.85 g of NaCl. Using Na = 23 and Cl = 35.5, M(NaCl) = 58.5 g/mol. Thus n = 5.85/58.5 = 0.100 mol. The formula mass must be calculated before using the conversion.

Try it yourself

Show your working or explain your reasoning, then open the answers to check.

  1. Find the amount in 9.0 g of water. Use H = 1 and O = 16.
  2. Convert 27 °C to Kelvin using T = °C + 273.
  3. A gas occupies 600 cm³ at 100 kPa. At constant temperature, find its volume at 150 kPa.
Show answers and working
  1. M(H₂O) = 18 g/mol, so n = 9.0/18 = 0.50 mol.
  2. T = 300 K.
  3. P₁V₁ = P₂V₂, so V₂ = 100 × 600/150 = 400 cm³.

Remember: For gas-law calculations involving temperature, use Kelvin. For a mass-to-moles calculation, keep the mass unit consistent with the molar mass.
