Free practice: Probability without replacement

After an item is removed without replacement, both the total number of items and the number of that kind may change. Use the updated counts for the second selection.

Worked example

A bag has 4 red and 3 blue counters. Two counters are drawn without replacement. P(both red) = 4/7 × 3/6 = 2/7. The denominator changes from 7 to 6 because the first counter stays out of the bag.

Try it yourself

Show your working or explain your reasoning, then open the answers to check.

  1. Two fair coins are tossed. Find P(exactly one head).
  2. A bag contains 3 red and 2 blue counters. Find P(red then blue), without replacement.
  3. If P(A) = 0.35, find P(not A).
Show answers and working
  1. The equally likely outcomes are HH, HT, TH and TT. Two qualify, so P = 2/4 = 1/2.
  2. 3/5 × 2/4 = 3/10.
  3. 1 − 0.35 = 0.65.

Remember: State whether items are replaced before multiplying probabilities. Drawing two red counters with replacement uses different numbers.
