Free practice: Calculating solution concentration

Amount concentration is c = n/V. When the answer is required in mol/dm³, convert the solution volume from cm³ to dm³ by dividing by 1,000.

Worked example

Dissolve 0.060 mol of solute to make 300 cm³ of solution. The volume is 0.300 dm³, so c = 0.060/0.300 = 0.20 mol/dm³. Use the final solution volume, rather than simply the volume of water added.

Try it yourself

Show your working or explain your reasoning, then open the answers to check.

  1. Calculate the concentration of 0.10 mol NaCl in 250 cm³ of solution.
  2. A reaction produces 90 cm³ of gas in 45 s. Find its average gas-production rate.
  3. Equal amounts of calcium carbonate powder and chips react with excess acid. Which reacts faster, and how do final gas volumes compare?
Show answers and working
  1. V = 0.250 dm³. c = 0.10/0.250 = 0.40 mol/dm³.
  2. Average rate = 90/45 = 2.0 cm³/s.
  3. Powder reacts faster because it has a larger exposed surface area. Equal amounts produce equal final CO₂ volumes under the same conditions.

Remember: Distinguish the rate of a reaction from the total amount of product. A faster reaction need not produce more gas.
