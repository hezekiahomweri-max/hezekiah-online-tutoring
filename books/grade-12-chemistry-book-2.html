Free practice: Reasoning about ethanol production

Fermentation converts glucose into ethanol and carbon dioxide. Suitable warm conditions and yeast are needed; oxygen is limited. Use a balanced equation to connect reactant and product amounts.

Worked example

C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ shows that 1 mol of glucose can produce 2 mol of ethanol. Thus 0.50 mol of glucose has a theoretical ethanol yield of 1.0 mol, assuming complete conversion by this reaction.

Try it yourself

Show your working or explain your reasoning, then open the answers to check.

  1. How many moles of CO₂ are produced theoretically from 0.25 mol glucose by fermentation?
  2. What organic product forms when ethanol is fully oxidised under suitable conditions?
  3. Why is combustion alone insufficient to identify ethanol?
Show answers and working
  1. The ratio glucose:CO₂ is 1:2, so 0.50 mol CO₂.
  2. Ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH.
  3. Other combustible organic substances also produce carbon dioxide and water. Additional chemical evidence is needed.

Remember: A theoretical yield assumes complete conversion. Actual production can be lower because of incomplete reaction and processing losses.
