Free practice: A horizontal projectile

With air resistance neglected, horizontal velocity stays constant while vertical motion accelerates under gravity. Solve the vertical part to find flight time, then calculate horizontal distance.

Worked example

A ball is projected horizontally at 10 m/s from a height of 20 m. Take g = 10 m/s². From h = ½gt², t = √(2 × 20/10) = 2 s. Horizontal distance is vt = 10 × 2 = 20 m.

Try it yourself

Show your working or explain your reasoning, then open the answers to check.

  1. A ball is projected horizontally at 12 m/s from 20 m above the ground. Use g = 10 m/s² and neglect air resistance. Find its range.
  2. What are the dimensions of acceleration?
  3. What is the horizontal acceleration of a projectile when air resistance is neglected?
Show answers and working
  1. The flight time is 2 s, so range = 12 × 2 = 24 m.
  2. Acceleration is change in velocity per time, so its dimensions are LT⁻².
  3. Zero: the only force in this model is vertical gravity.

Remember: A horizontal launch starts with zero vertical velocity, but its horizontal velocity is not zero.
