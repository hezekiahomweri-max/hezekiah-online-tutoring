Free Chemistry questions with answers: acids and bases

Attempt these questions first. Then open the answers and correct your reasoning.

  1. Three aqueous solutions have pH values of 3, 7 and 11 at room temperature. Classify each as acidic, neutral or alkaline.
  2. Magnesium reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid. Write a balanced equation and name the gas produced.
  3. Calcium carbonate reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid. Write a balanced equation and describe how the gas can be identified.
  4. A farmer measures soil pH 4.5. The intended crop grows best near pH 6.5. Suggest a treatment and a follow-up measurement.
  5. A learner compares the reaction of magnesium with hydrochloric acid at different concentrations. Identify the independent variable, two controlled variables and one measurable outcome.
Show worked answers
  1. pH 3: acidic; pH 7: neutral; pH 11: alkaline.
  2. Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g). The gas is hydrogen.
  3. CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g). Carbon dioxide turns limewater milky when bubbled through it.
  4. Apply an appropriate amount of agricultural lime, guided by soil-test recommendations, to reduce soil acidity. Measure soil pH again to check progress towards the crop’s suitable range.
  5. Independent variable: acid concentration. Keep temperature and the mass and surface area of magnesium constant; also use the same volume of acid. Measure the volume of hydrogen produced in a fixed time using suitable apparatus. Repeat trials to check reliability.

Hint: an observation describes what happens; an explanation uses chemical ideas to account for it.

Remember: acid strength and concentration describe different things. A weak acid is not necessarily dilute.

Caution: perform chemical experiments under teacher or laboratory supervision, with eye protection and appropriate apparatus. Keep ignition sources away from hydrogen during gas collection.
