Free practice: Simplifying and combining surds

Extract square factors first. Then combine only terms with the same irrational factor; √2 and √3 cannot be added as like terms.

Worked example

Simplify 2√12 + √27 − √3. Since √12 = 2√3 and √27 = 3√3, the expression becomes 4√3 + 3√3 − √3 = 6√3.

Try it yourself

Show your working or explain your reasoning, then open the answers to check.

  1. Simplify √50 + √8.
  2. Rationalise 3/√3.
  3. Solve 2x² + x − 6 = 0.
Show answers and working
  1. 5√2 + 2√2 = 7√2.
  2. Multiply numerator and denominator by √3: 3√3/3 = √3.
  3. (2x − 3)(x + 2) = 0, hence x = 3/2 or −2.

Remember: Check factorisation by expanding your brackets. A correct pair of roots must satisfy the original quadratic equation.
