Free practice: Reading measurements with units

A measurement needs a number and a unit. Read a measuring cylinder at eye level; for water, use the bottom of the meniscus to reduce parallax error.

Worked example

A learner writes a water-volume reading as “35”. The record is incomplete because the unit is missing. Writing “35 mL” identifies the measured quantity clearly; 35 mL is equal to 35 cm³.

Try it yourself

Attempt the questions before opening the answers.

  1. Convert 20 mL to cm³.
  2. Why should the eye be level with the liquid surface when reading a scale?
  3. What happens when two north poles of magnets are brought together?
Show answers and working
  1. 20 cm³, since 1 mL = 1 cm³.
  2. To reduce parallax error from viewing the scale at an angle.
  3. They repel.

Remember: Select an instrument suited to the quantity. A balance measures mass; a measuring cylinder measures liquid volume.
